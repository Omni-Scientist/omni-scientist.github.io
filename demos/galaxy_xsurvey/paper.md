## Title
Consistent Morphological Classifications from a General-Purpose Vision-Language Model Across Deep and Shallow Imaging of the Same Galaxies

## Abstract
Automated morphological classification of galaxies traditionally relies on purpose-built machine learning models trained on labeled imaging datasets, raising questions about whether general-purpose vision-language models (VLMs) could offer a more flexible alternative. It remains unclear whether such models' outputs are driven by genuine morphological features or are instead highly sensitive to image quality and survey depth. We examined paired galaxy images from DECaLS, a deep imaging survey, and SDSS, a shallower survey, applying a general-purpose VLM to assign morphological labels to each image independently. The main finding is that the VLM's classifications show high agreement between the two surveys, far exceeding what chance alone would predict. This consistency indicates that the model's labeling behavior is largely stable across differences in image depth and resolution rather than being an artifact of imaging quality. The result suggests that VLM-based morphological labels are reproducible under this detector across heterogeneous survey conditions, rather than reflecting noise-driven instability. These findings support the feasibility of using general-purpose VLMs as consistent, survey-agnostic tools for large-scale galaxy morphology cataloguing, pending further validation against expert-labeled ground truth.
